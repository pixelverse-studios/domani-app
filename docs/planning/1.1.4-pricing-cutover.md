# 1.1.4 lifetime-price cutover (DEV-1555)

Status: implementation plan; **do not change production pricing yet**.

## Current risk

- `signup_cohort` is not price eligibility: production already has pre-cutover accounts in the `general` cohort.
- `public_pricing` still advertises the early-adopter amount, while `general` routes to the standard RevenueCat offering.
- `profiles` has a user-owned update policy. A writable profile column cannot be the authoritative grandfathering record.
- A missing selected RevenueCat offering must not fall back to another offering. The first code change on this ticket fails closed here.

## Server contract and cutover

Implement after DEV-1415 establishes the canonical migration sequence and coordinate with DEV-1418/DEV-1419 before deployment:

1. Persist one immutable cutover timestamp in server-owned configuration. The user-scoped eligibility contract compares it with `auth.users.created_at`, not an editable profile field or the broad signup cohort. Clients cannot edit the timestamp or their authoritative account creation time.
2. Make the cutover an explicit, one-time, audited operation. Record the timestamp and count accounts created before it, including active, expired, and never-started trials in every cohort. Every pre-cutover account keeps the $9.99 offer without an expiration date; accounts created at or after cutover get $34.99. A single timestamp comparison avoids missing an account created concurrently with cutover.
3. The eligibility read returns only `early_adopter`, `general`, or unavailable. Reinstall and account switching must fetch the signed-in account's result anew. Offline or failed verification returns unavailable, never a guessed discount.
4. Trial extensions do not change price eligibility. Existing accounts retain the $9.99 offer after trial expiration; trial access and lifetime purchase access remain separate from price eligibility.
5. Existing lifetime purchases and promo grants bypass the paywall and retain their access. Restoration and refunds continue to use the established entitlement sync; pricing eligibility must never itself confer access.

## App and store integration

- Keep separate $9.99 and $34.99 store products and matching RevenueCat offerings on both platforms. Verify their identifiers and availability in sandbox before exposing the new route.
- Select the exact offering from the server result. Do not substitute RevenueCat's current offering if that identifier is absent. Display `product.priceString` for paywall and purchase confirmation; disable purchase and avoid an amount when eligibility or the matching product is unavailable.
- Align public login/onboarding copy with the verified standard store product and cutover config. Remove the persisted early-adopter fallback; an unavailable or stale config/catalog should show price-neutral copy rather than an unverified amount. Account for apps left open across cutover, not only fresh launches.
- Record non-PII analytics for offer exposure, checkout start, successful purchase, and offer/price mismatch with platform and offer identifier. Do not log payment tokens or account identifiers in analytics.

## Rollout and rollback gates

1. Merge and replay the reconciled migrations in staging; verify this contract with new, active-trial, expired-trial, never-started-trial, lifetime, and promo accounts on iOS and Android.
2. Verify both store products and RevenueCat offerings, including localized prices, purchase, restore, refund, and account-switching flows. Verify how every still-supported app version routes purchases; provide a compatible server-enforced path or require an update before cutover. Store approval alone does not update installed apps.
3. Deploy the compatible app and inactive server contract. Make public signup copy price-neutral before activation, including cached or foregrounded sessions, and verify that no supported client still advertises an unverified $9.99 price. Audit the count of all existing accounts immediately before cutover.
4. Activate the immutable timestamp and `public_pricing=standard` as one coordinated cutover, with no user-visible interval where new accounts qualify for $34.99 while public copy advertises $9.99. Verify new and pre-cutover account prices on both platforms, audit eligible counts and account samples, and monitor price-mismatch events. If the two changes cannot be made atomically, keep public copy price-neutral until both are confirmed.
5. If validation fails, pause new exposure and restore price-neutral public copy/config. Do not rewrite the cutover timestamp or reassign existing purchasers. Reverting the store-facing offer requires separate review because purchases and cutover eligibility are durable.

No production migration, RevenueCat dashboard change, or `public_pricing` flip is part of this preparatory change.
